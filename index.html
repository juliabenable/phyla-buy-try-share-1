Why returns are a bigger risk here than on Aisle

decided Julia and Katie chose not to run returns checks in the pilot. The analysis below stays for the 100-creator run.

my analysis Not raised on the call. Full reimbursement doubles the payoff of a return compared with Aisle's 50% cashback. A creator who returns the bottle after being paid back keeps the full price.

OfferCreator paysGets back from the brandGets back from Target on returnNet if they return it
Aisle, $15 off$55$15$55+$15 = 15 + 55 − 55
Aisle, 50% back$55$27.50$55+$27.50 = 27.50 + 55 − 55
Buy, Try, Share$55$55$55+$55 = 55 + 55 − 55

Before tax. $55 = Phyla serum price at Target (Oct 21 study). Aisle offers are as Gianna described them today; she said the 50% offer saw 80 to 90% returned, and on Nov 9 she gave ~80% on the cleanser.

What works in our favor: the creator has to post a video of themselves using it before getting a cent, and they lose access to every future paid and gifted collab on Benable. Gianna said on the call that Aisle has the same rule, and both she and Tony said creators who want brand collabs should care about it more than deal hunters do. The pilot is how we find out.

Can a receipt show a return? Not alone, but Phyla's store data can

Tony asked this in Slack today. research unless marked; only the second and last points come from the call.

  • No public lookup. Target's help pages describe receipt lookup only at the store, for the customer's own return. Nothing lets a brand or a third party check a receipt number for a refund.
  • from the call Phyla already gets returns data. Gianna said today they check a Target returns sheet every Sunday, with numbers for the prior week, and that Aisle matches returns to the receipts it collects, down to people who bought and returned on the same day.
  • What Target gives brands is counts per item, per store, per period, not receipts. Brands see sales through Target's supplier portal, Partners Online, and its analytics tool, Greenfield. Third-party guides describe sales by store and by week, some daily. Target's own published sales-data file spec (EDI 852) reports quantities per item per store per period, with codes for inventory, on order and quantity sold. It has no receipt or transaction field and no separate returns code. That spec dates from 2000, so the current feed may differ, but nothing found suggests brands get receipt-level data.
  • So the sheet probably shows returned units by store and day or week. That is still enough for Aisle-style matching, because Phyla sells so few units per store (below).
  • The receipt number itself carries the store and date. According to a Target service desk trainer on a Target employee forum (unverified), the REC# reads: receipt type, date (day of year), store number, register, transaction number, check digit, and every online order uses store number 3991. So we can read the store and date straight off the receipt and catch any receipt number used twice.
  • from the call Opened skincare can still go back. Gianna said so today. Third-party guides cite 90 days for opened beauty with a receipt; Target's own page only says opened items may be denied. Treat the window as long.

What this means for paying creators (if returns checks come back later)

Payment already waits for the post, and the post is due within 10 days of buying. So by the time most creators get paid, the next Sunday returns sheet may already cover their purchase day. proposed Pay on post as agreed, but if no returns sheet has covered the purchase date yet, wait for the next one. That adds at most 7 days and catches the same-day and next-day returns Aisle saw.
